# T4 — Evidence plan for a publishable WBR paper

## Scope and evidence boundary

The paper should make one testable claim, such as: *a low-cost perception-to-MCU advisory can estimate obstacle geometry and deliver a correctly classified, freshness-bounded advisory under specified conditions*. It should not imply autonomous jumping, improved balance, or safe motor control. The STM32 retains balance and actuator authority. Define the primary claim and acceptance limits before collecting confirmatory trials.

The repository already has useful simulation, recorded camera, protocol, and replay infrastructure. Treat reported numbers as evidence only for their stated setup. The expected WBR hardware arrives in November 2026; robot trials below are therefore future work.

## Experiments before the robot exists

1. **Lock protocol and claim.** Specify supported obstacle classes, height/range bins, lateral offsets, lighting/floor conditions, output states, stale timeout, and the controller-independent advisory semantics. Set thresholds from the intended use and calibration uncertainty. Freeze code/config hashes, splits, exclusions, and primary metrics before confirmatory data.
2. **Build a physical camera dataset.** Use measured boxes/steps with surveyed height, width, front distance, lateral offset, camera height and pitch. Include empty-floor negatives, out-of-corridor objects, thresholds, textured/reflective surfaces, shadows, floor transitions, and depth holes. Capture multiple sessions with changed placement and lighting; retain raw bags, calibration, metadata, reference measurements, and failed captures. Split by scene/session/object placement, not random adjacent frames, to prevent leakage.
3. **Compare perception baselines.** At minimum: (a) current per-frame plane + geometry + temporal filter; (b) geometry without temporal filtering; (c) depth-only height/range baseline or fixed threshold; and (d) an explicitly defined ground-truth geometry oracle as an upper bound where meaningful. Use identical frames, calibration and thresholds chosen on training/validation data. Report per-class precision/recall, confusion matrix, false advisory rate per negative scene/minute, abstention/UNCERTAIN rate, height and distance MAE/median/P95, and coverage versus error. Do not optimize thresholds on test scenes.
4. **Ablate mechanisms.** Remove temporal confirmation/hysteresis; remove plane reuse or compare with no plane reuse; vary depth noise/hole and camera pitch/height within measured bounds; compare single-frame and short-window policies. Keep one factor changed at a time and label exploratory sweeps. Report failures, not just successful detections.
5. **Measure compute and link independently.** On the Pi, record monotonic capture/arrival, processing start/end, publish, serial enqueue/transmit, and MCU receive/decision timestamps with clock-domain mapping and uncertainty. Measure p50/p95/p99/max latency, jitter, dropped frames, queue age, UART RTT, CRC errors, loss, duplicate/reordered frames, and stale rejection at 300 ms. Inject delay, burst loss, corruption, and producer stall. Confirm FRESH→HOLD→FAILSAFE transitions and recovery with a hardware-in-loop or host MCU harness; the receiver must reject expired or malformed advisories. Keep sensor-to-decision age distinct from RTT.
6. **Repeat and quantify uncertainty.** For perception, target at least 30 independent placements per primary class/bin across at least 3 separately reset sessions, with scene/session as the sampling unit; expand based on a pilot-derived precision or interval-width calculation. Adjacent frames from one stationary placement are repeated measures, not independent trials. For link stress, use at least 10 independent runs per condition and enough messages to estimate rare failures; zero failures in a finite sample is not a zero failure probability. State the denominator and exact binomial interval. Report per-session results and pooled estimates with 95% confidence intervals, clustered bootstrap by scene/session or an appropriate mixed-effects model. Predeclare primary endpoint, multiplicity handling, and exclusions.

## Experiments after the robot arrives

1. **Characterize hardware before closed-loop use.** Record actual camera-to-body extrinsics, camera pose/mount repeatability, MCU/Pi clock relation, CAN and micro-ROS timing, motor limits and delays, sensor rates, and robot mass/COM from documented measurements. Run perception while the robot is stationary and manually repositioned; compare geometry against surveyed targets. Do not substitute the assumed planar model for measured parameters.
2. **Moving-platform perception.** Repeat the frozen obstacle/negative dataset at safe, explicitly bounded robot postures and motions. Measure advisory classification and age against independent ground truth. Include vibration, body pitch/roll, wheel motion, lighting and floor conditions. Separate camera-frame geometry accuracy from any transformed body/world estimate.
3. **Advisory/MCU integration without motor authority.** Verify message validity, freshness, state transitions, watchdog behavior, and non-interference while the MCU continues its existing controller. Inject stale/corrupt/delayed traffic and Pi/process loss. Capture synchronized logs from camera through MCU decision. No jump or obstacle-crossing claim follows from successful message delivery.
4. **If claiming task utility, run a separately approved supervised robot study.** Compare a predeclared baseline (no advisory / fixed conservative stop) against the advisory-enabled high-level policy, with the balance controller, operator intervention rules, and physical safety boundary fixed. Start with non-contact/static targets and progression criteria. Count robot/session as the unit where appropriate; randomize order, use multiple sessions and obstacle placements, and log every abort. Report completion, false-stop/missed-obstacle rates, intervention/abort counts, minimum clearance and timing with intervals. A handful of successful demonstrations is illustrative, not statistical validation.

## Threats to validity to address

- **Construct:** tabletop boxes and geometric classes do not represent all terrain or prove jumpability; advisory labels are not motor permissions.
- **Internal:** calibration drift, hand-measured ground truth, camera pitch, synchronized frame selection, threshold tuning, temporal leakage, and unrecorded scene exclusions can bias performance. Keep a sealed session-level test set and full provenance.
- **Statistical:** repeated frames inflate sample size; rare false negatives need large denominators; one robot and few sessions limit generalization. Give intervals and counts, not only averages or pass rates.
- **External:** one D435i/Pi, one mount, indoor lighting/floor, and one WBR cannot establish other cameras, outdoor use, robots, or terrain. State environmental envelope.
- **Timing:** ROS/header clocks, device time and MCU monotonic time may differ; bag replay does not include live transport. Quantify synchronization error and distinguish processing latency, one-way age, and RTT.
- **Model/control:** the planar plant and assumed parameters do not establish actual dynamic margin or safe obstacle crossing. Keep perception/protocol claims separate from controller efficacy unless robot trials directly test it.
- **Implementation:** synthetic tests and host tests do not demonstrate target scheduling, worst-case execution time, serial/CAN interactions, or fault containment. Report target build/config and stress conditions.

## Recommended paper evidence sequence

Use pre-robot work to establish a defensible physical-camera geometry dataset, frozen baseline/ablations, Pi timing, and stale-message fault handling. After arrival, add measured extrinsics and moving-platform/MCU integration. Make the first paper about measured perception and freshness-bounded advisory performance. Add robot task utility only if a controlled, safe study provides adequate independent trials; otherwise present integration as a bounded feasibility demonstration and leave benefit/safety claims for later work.
