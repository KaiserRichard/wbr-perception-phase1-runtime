# T2 — Freshness contracts from perception to a safety controller

**Scope/date:** literature and repository scan, 2026-10-30. This is a scouting assessment, not a systematic review or a safety certification claim.

## Finding

Age of Information (AoI) measures how old the newest information available to a receiver is, commonly as current time minus the generation time of the newest received update. For a control application, the more meaningful quantity can be **actuation-side AoI**: age of the sensor observation that underlies a command when that command is applied. It includes sensing, perception, queues, transport, MCU scheduling and actuation, rather than reporting link round-trip time alone.

Freshness matters because a delivered value may still be obsolete. Reliability and freshness therefore differ: retransmitting an old advisory can increase delivery probability while making its control value worse. A defensible contract needs source timestamp or bounded source age, sequence/session, validity/health, a maximum age, and an explicit expiration action. If clocks are not comparable, use a single monotonic clock or carry a conservative age bound; do not silently subtract unsynchronized clocks.

The closest prior work is Jeon, Mtowe and Kim (2026): a camera-based networked robot testbed, compute offloading, and actuation-side AoI measured at the motors. This directly occupies broad “perception freshness affects robot control” territory. It is accepted in *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* (formatted version pending at access time), DOI 10.3389/frobt.2026.1999591. A separate ROS 2 timing paper formally analyzes data-refreshing chains and end-to-end age/latency; ROS 2 itself already provides deadline, liveliness and lifespan QoS. Runtime monitoring of ROS 2 requirements is also established. Thus “we add freshness monitoring” or “we use AoI in robotics” is not a novel claim.

**Assessment:** “perception advisory with freshness contract to a safety MCU” is potentially publishable only when framed as an end-to-end implementation and evidence contribution: a semantic, fail-closed lease across the Pi/ROS-to-MCU boundary, with measured observation-to-decision age and controlled fault injection showing that expired advice cannot regain authority through buffering, reconnect, replay or sequence wrap. The architecture alone is not new enough. Differentiation from the 2026 robot paper must be concrete: theirs studies edge computation placement and camera-control actuation AoI; a WBR paper would need to study the boundary between non-safety perception advice and an independently authoritative embedded safety controller, including expiration semantics and fallback behavior.

## Relevant prior work and platform facts

- **Direct freshness/AoI robotics precedent:** Jeon, Junyeong; Daniel Poul Mtowe; Dong Min Kim. “Computation Offloading and Information Freshness in Edge-Assisted Networked Robotic Systems.” *Frontiers in Robotics and AI*, 13 (2026), DOI: [10.3389/frobt.2026.1999591](https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2026.1999591). **Opened.** Physical road-following testbed; computes observation age at motor actuation and compares four computation cut points under two uplink conditions. Closest prior work; novelty risk is **very high** for generic perception-to-control AoI and offloading claims.
- **ROS 2 processing-chain timing:** Tang, Yue; Xu Jiang; Nan Guan; Xiantong Luo; Maolin Yang; Wang Yi. “Timing analysis of processing chains with data refreshing in ROS 2.” *Journal of Systems Architecture*, 155 (2024), 103259. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysarc.2024.103259). **Opened.** Formal timing analysis for refreshing semantics, latency and buffer sizing. Distinguish an empirical cross-device contract/fault study from this analytical work; do not claim the first ROS 2 freshness analysis.
- **ROS 2 runtime verification:** Perez, Ivan; Anastasia Mavridou; Tom Pressburger; Alexander Will; Patrick J. Martin. “Monitoring ROS2: from Requirements to Autonomous Robots.” *Electronic Proceedings in Theoretical Computer Science*, 2022, FMAS 2022. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14030). **Opened.** Translates requirements into temporal-logic monitors and generated ROS 2 monitor packages. The existence of runtime monitors is established; a contribution must focus on an application-specific contract, cross-boundary realization and measured effect.
- **ROS 2 QoS design:** Open Robotics, “Quality of Service settings: deadline, liveliness, lifespan,” ROS 2 design article (2018). [Design article](https://github.com/ros2/design/blob/gh-pages/articles/qos_deadline_liveliness_lifespan.md). **Opened.** Deadline detects a missed periodic-update contract, liveliness indicates entity/process presence, and lifespan prevents delivery after sample expiration. These middleware features do not by themselves prove that a particular advisory is semantically valid, that its camera observation is recent, or that the MCU has entered a safe state. QoS defaults include history depth 10; stale queued samples deserve explicit attention.
- **micro-ROS transport:** eProsima, “eProsima Micro XRCE-DDS Client,” Micro XRCE-DDS documentation (living docs, version accessed 2026-10-30). [Client docs](https://github.com/eProsima/Micro-XRCE-DDS-docs/blob/master/docs/client.rst). **Opened.** Client-Agent communication supports best-effort and reliable streams. Reliable delivery and retries are transport semantics, not an application freshness guarantee; timeout, session restart, message age and fail-safe policy remain system design obligations.

## What the existing WBR evidence supports

- The brief reports 630/630 UART echoes and RTT P50 2.83 ms for the Pi-to-MCU link, plus a terrain advisory with NONE / JUMP_CANDIDATE / STOP / UNCERTAIN and a 300 ms stale-after rule. These establish an encouraging communication/advisory baseline, not end-to-end observation age or motor-applied AoI.
- The 2026-10-24 local review describes a 42-byte/27-byte protocol, CRC and sequence/session rejection, plus a 400 ms silence/recovery exercise in simulation. It explicitly says physical Nucleo/Pi UART, physical USART2, analyzer capture, hardware latency/loss and physical behavior are **UNVERIFIED**; no hardware was flashed. Do not write as though this is already a verified MCU safety response.
- The software boundary is sound as a research premise: the STM32 owns balance and actuator safety; Pi vision advice is advisory. Keep that boundary intact in experiments and claims. A valid communication packet is not evidence that perception is correct or a jump is safe.

## Candidate paper ideas

1. **Cross-layer freshness lease for perception advisories on a resource-limited robot.** Define an advisory validity lease tied to capture time/age, monotonically checked sequence/session and explicit expiry; MCU locally rejects expired, duplicate, replayed and invalid data and stays in its independently safe policy. Measure capture-to-MCU-decision age distribution, rejection latency, CPU/RAM/bytes, and outcomes under delay, burst loss, reorder, duplication, stale replay, reset and reconnect. Compare (a) reliable transport only, (b) receipt-time timeout, and (c) observation-age lease with MCU rejection. **Novelty risk: medium-high** because of ROS 2 QoS/runtime verification and the 2026 actuation-AoI paper. Strongest if real MCU and robot evidence shows a tested contract across the asynchronous boundary.
2. **Freshness-aware advisory usefulness and safe fallback under perception/compute stalls.** Replay synchronized camera/terrain sequences while injecting CPU overload, delayed frames and tracking/quality invalidation. Compare fixed 300 ms timeout to a task-conditioned maximum age derived from obstacle closing speed / stopping or jump-decision horizon, and to no advisory. Report stale-but-plausible advisory rate, missed/false candidate rate, age at decision, fallback coverage and confidence intervals. Keep the MCU’s core safety independent. **Novelty risk: high** for “adaptive AoI” alone; lower if grounded in a transparent physical hazard bound and validates the contract against real sensing/MCU timing.
3. **When does reliable delivery hurt freshness in a perception-to-MCU channel?** Bench best-effort/latest-value versus reliable/retrying link policies with sequence replacement, bounded queues and explicit expiration. Sweep loss, delay and MCU scheduling load; plot deadline-miss probability and AoI tail (p95/p99/max) jointly with delivery rate and stale-use count. **Novelty risk: high** in general networking, but potentially useful as an embedded robotics design result when tested on the actual serial/micro-ROS path and mapped to safe semantics.

## Minimum credible evidence and recommendation

Use synchronized monotonic timestamps at image acquisition, perception completion, Pi transmit, MCU receive, MCU decision and (if safely observable) controller consumption. Report calibration/error bounds for timestamp mapping; mean alone is insufficient—include p95/p99, maximum, age exceedance rate, loss/reorder/reconnect cases and confidence intervals over repeated runs. Include workloads that contend for Pi CPU and UART, and disclose the test envelope. Separate **transport liveness**, **sample freshness**, **perception validity**, **advisory policy**, and **actuator safety** in the design and results.

Do not sell “an AoI paper” or “a ROS freshness monitor.” The strongest path is candidate 1 with candidate 2’s task-conditioned expiration rationale, after real MCU implementation is exercised with non-actuating fault injection and then evaluated on the arriving robot. Until physical evidence exists, pitch it as a software/bench prototype and keep all hardware safety claims explicitly unverified.
