# X2 — RL for wheel-legged / wheel-biped locomotion

**Scope:** research map and feasible path for our WBR, not a training authorization. Sources below were opened; compute figures are source-specific and not apples-to-apples.

## 1. State of the art: selected works

- **“Learning robust autonomous navigation and locomotion for wheeled-legged robots” (Lee et al., 2024, *Science Robotics* 9(89), eadi9641).** Strongest morphology match: privileged-learning locomotion with driving/walking transitions, obstacle negotiation (including jumps off tables), and hierarchical navigation; validated on kilometer-scale urban missions. Their robot, actuator stack, and compute are unlike ours, so transfer is a method reference, not a ready policy. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01792) · [project/code](https://junja94.github.io/learning_robust_autonomous_navigation_and_locomotion_for_wheeled_legged_robots/)
- **“Control of Wheeled-Legged Quadrupeds Using Deep Reinforcement Learning” (Lee, Bjelonic & Hutter, 2022, CLAWAR; published in *Robotics in Natural Settings*, 2023).** Sim-trained policy deployed on a quadruped and learned driving/stepping mode switches. Useful early direct precedent; less integrated than the 2024 work. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-15226-9_14) · [conference program](https://clawar.org/clawar2022/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CLAWAR2022_Programme_Booklet.pdf)
- **“Sim2Biped: Enhancing Mobility of a Wheeled Biped via Sim-to-Real Transfer of an RL Controller” (Levine & Sun, Stanford CS224R course project, year/venue UNVERIFIED).** Morphology is unusually close: PPO plus terrain curriculum improved tested ledge traversal from 14 mm to 37 mm and ramp slope from 0.1 to 0.25 versus its linear baseline; tests were small (at least 3 trials/condition), and policy had no vision. Treat as a useful project report, not peer-reviewed evidence. [Report](https://selenasun.com/papers/cs224r.pdf)
- **“Learning robust perceptive locomotion for quadrupedal robots in the wild” (Miki et al., 2022, *Science Robotics*).** Relevant teacher–student pattern: train with privileged terrain information, then distill/use onboard perception under imperfect height estimates. Quadruped results do not establish benefit on our wheel-biped. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abk2822)
- **“RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots” (Kumar et al., 2021, Robotics: Science and Systems).** Teacher/adaptation module infers latent dynamics such as payload and ground condition from observation history; sim-trained policy transferred to ANYmal A1 without real-world fine-tuning. A candidate for actuator/friction variation after a baseline is stable, not the first WBR policy. [Paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p011.pdf) · [code/project](https://ashish-kmr.github.io/rma-legged-robots/)
- **“Learning to Walk in Minutes Using Massively Parallel Deep Reinforcement Learning” (Rudin et al., 2021, CoRL / PMLR 164).** GPU-parallel PPO and curriculum transferred to ANYmal; paper reports under 4 min for flat terrain and 20 min uneven terrain on its workstation GPU setup. This is an optimized GPU result, not an expected Mac/Pi runtime. [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/rudin22a.html) · [Legged Gym code](https://github.com/leggedrobotics/legged_gym)
- **“Learning Agile Robotic Locomotion Skills by Imitating Animals” (Peng et al., 2020, RSS).** Shows learned hops and dynamic motions on a quadruped from motion imitation. Useful for the jumping literature, but jumping is a later, higher-risk target for our two-legged morphology. [Paper](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss16/p064.pdf)
- **Training stacks:** [MuJoCo Playground](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_playground) (2025 technical report; MJX/JAX GPU route with sim-to-real examples), [Isaac Lab](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab) (active NVIDIA GPU-centric framework), and [Genesis](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis) (open-source general simulation/robot-learning framework). These are tools, not evidence that a WBR model or policy transfers.

## 2. Visible “wow” demo

Best credible demo: command a forward speed; the robot rolls across flat floor, detects a deliberately chosen low step from a short-range height/elevation representation, slows, raises/steps its legs while keeping the body upright, then resumes rolling. Show the predicted terrain profile, chosen gait/mode, and IMU/body-angle trace beside video. An identical LQR or hand-coded controller is the baseline. Begin with a secured low step and tether/overhead catch; do not make a jump the first physical demo. “Learns to jump stairs” is visually stronger but not presently supportable: robot, torque-speed/thermal limits, impacts, and 3D model are not yet measured.

## 3. What is researchable for us

A defensible question is: **does a privileged height-map teacher with a deployable, noisy D435i/student height-map improve step negotiation over the same proprioceptive policy, at matched training budget, versus LQR and a non-learning mode-switch baseline?** Score completion, falls, peak body pitch/roll, time, energy/current proxy, commanded/actual mode transitions, and uncertainty/fallback frequency over held-out step heights, approach speeds, friction, payload, and sensor dropout. Use repeated training seeds and paired physical trials; preregister limits and retain failures.

A potential contribution is the *timing-aware perception-to-control contract*: estimate age/error of the local height map, test delay/dropout explicitly, and let a conservative supervisor reject stale/uncertain maps. Novelty is **not established**; related perceptive locomotion, terrain mapping, and wheel-legged control already exist. First compare to Lee et al. (2024), Miki et al. (2022), and the 2019–2022 wheel-legged MPC/RL literature. Separate a publishable systems result (measured end-to-end age, intervention/failure rates) from any claim that RL itself is better.

**Task order:** balance/recovery on measured flat floor → roll/drive speed tracking → low step ascent/descent → teacher–student with onboard height map → disturbances/payload and delay generalization → only then jump/hop. Keep a deterministic MCU safety envelope and existing LQR fallback. Policy inference can run on Pi or MCU only after worst-case timing, numeric format, and safe output limits are measured; STM32 retains balance and motor safety.

## 4. Feasibility on our hardware

**Now:** Intel Mac has no NVIDIA CUDA GPU; it can develop/inspect MuJoCo models and run small CPU experiments, but is a poor choice for thousands of parallel 3D PPO environments. The current six-state planar MuJoCo plant has two paired inputs, no lateral/roll/yaw motion, no contact/terrain or five-bar geometry. It cannot answer stair, jump, height-map, or full-body sim-to-real questions. Existing PPO v2 underperformed its frozen LQR reference (59/800 direct successes; residual 0/480); the v3 pilot failed its frozen screens and is stopped. Treat this as a warning against claiming a working WBR RL baseline; do not tune/retry it under this brief.

Pi 5 (8 GB, CPU-only for training) is for onboard sensing/inference and ROS integration, not large-scale RL training. Hailo-8L/AI HAT+ has 13 TOPS INT8 for supported inference, but does not accelerate MuJoCo/PPO training and is not needed for a small proprioceptive MLP. D435i can provide step geometry; the 24 Oct RGB-D box bags are not locomotion or step-ground-truth data and do not train a locomotion policy.

For scale, an illustrative actor with 32 inputs, two 128-unit hidden layers, and 12 outputs has about 22,000 weights (~88 kB in FP32; under 45,000 multiply-accumulates per action). It is a small inference workload, unlike simulation/training. No verified apples-to-apples FPS for this policy on our Intel Mac, Pi 5 CPU, Hailo-8L, or Orin Nano was found; benchmark end-to-end at the chosen control rate, including ROS, sensors, and worst-case scheduling. The Hailo TOPS number is not a policy-FPS estimate.

**After arrival:** STM32H723 + BMI088 + DM-J4310 hip and DM-H6215 wheel motors over CAN + five-bar legs make low-level policy execution conceivable. Pi-to-MCU advisory uses COBS+CRC UART, with no micro-ROS. First obtain exact CAD/masses/inertias, linkage geometry, actuator torque-speed/current/thermal curves, encoder/IMU rates and filters, CAN/UART timing, wheel/contact data, and control limits. Build the 3D MuJoCo MJCF from CAD/URDF: floating base (x,y,z, roll,pitch,yaw), both linked legs, wheels and ground contacts; implement measured actuator saturation, rate, delay, motor dynamics, sensor/filter timing, and controller update schedule. Validate static balance, kinematics, actuator sign/torque response, and short recorded open-loop trajectories before RL. Model five-bar closure with a verified constraint or explicit linkage and measured inertias; do not invent equivalent hip torques and call it the robot.

For teacher–student: teacher gets true simulated terrain heights/velocities and randomized physical parameters; student gets only deployable IMU/encoders/commands plus a local D435i-derived height patch with realistic occlusion, range, timestamp age, and noise. Distill teacher actions/latent estimates to student, then evaluate student alone. A less sensor-heavy first stage can use privileged terrain for training while actor remains proprioceptive, as in perceptive-locomotion work; it cannot anticipate unseen steps.

## 5. Compute to request

Request **one Linux workstation or lab/cloud allocation with NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB VRAM) or equivalent, 64 GB RAM, and 24–48 GPU-hours** for model integration, profiling, a pilot, and at least five-seed comparisons. Isaac Lab’s published benchmark lists 4,096 rough-terrain G1 environments at 94k environment steps/s and 82k/s with inference and training on RTX 4090; memory table reports 6.1 GB VRAM for that state-based task. Separate Isaac/Spot example reports 85–95k FPS on RTX 4090 for 4,096 environments, about four hours. These are reference ceilings/examples, not WBR estimates. Plan roughly **50–200M aggregate simulation transitions per experiment family** (including seeds/ablations); at 82k steps/s that is a raw 10–41 minutes, but model setup, task-specific throughput, evaluation, rendering, and failed runs dominate. The 24–48 GPU-hours is a planning allowance, not a measured requirement. [Isaac Lab benchmark](https://docs.nvidia.com/isaaclab/2.2/overview/performance-benchmarks) · [Spot training report](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/closing-the-sim-to-real-gap-training-spot-quadruped-locomotion-with-nvidia-isaac-lab)

Hailo-8L (13 TOPS) is inference-only for supported networks; Raspberry Pi currently lists AI HAT+ at 13/26 TOPS and AI HAT+ 2 at 40 TOPS, also inference, not RL simulation. Jetson Orin Nano is an edge inference option if ROS/perception needs GPU support; it is not a substitute for a workstation training GPU. Request an accelerator only after profiling the D435i pipeline and measured WBR CPU load. [Raspberry Pi AI HAT specs](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/ai-hat-plus.html)

## 6. Two-week prototype plan (research phase; no robot assumption)

**Days 1–2 — freeze scope/evidence:** preserve current STOPPED PPO results and LQR baseline; define low-step task, safety envelope, success/failure metrics, units, and five-seed protocol. Do not edit the owner-only gain schedule.

**Days 3–5 — 3D model skeleton:** after robot CAD/BOM and actuator data arrive, create a separate MuJoCo model with base, wheel contact, two leg joints/linkage, actuator limits/delay, and IMU/encoder observations. Before arrival, use a clearly labelled dimensioned placeholder only to test file plumbing; no claims about WBR dynamics.

**Days 6–7 — model checks:** verify gravity/static pose, joint signs/ranges, wheel rolling/contact, base-frame/IMU conventions, torque steps, and timing against hardware specs or measured logs. Render controlled roll/step trajectories. If CAD or motor data are missing, stop at a parameterized model and report the blocking unknowns.

**Days 8–10 — learning pipeline dry run:** on requested NVIDIA GPU, run a short PPO smoke and throughput/memory measurement on a flat command-tracking task; verify deterministic checkpoint reload and logs. Then train a low-step curriculum pilot. No transfer or performance conclusion from a smoke run.

**Days 11–12 — matched evaluation:** at minimum compare frozen LQR/mode-switch baseline and PPO on the same randomized flat/step cases, seeds, and time budget; retain all failures and held-out conditions. Add teacher height input only if base model and student observation pipeline work.

**Days 13–14 — review package:** publish model provenance/assumptions, raw configs/checkpoints/traces, throughput, five-seed distributions, plots, failure video, and sim-to-real gap checklist. If robot hardware has not arrived, deliver simulation-only status and a bench validation plan; do not claim physical transfer.

### Sources / limits

The papers establish demonstrations on their own robots and setups, not expected WBR performance. Published GPU FPS cannot be translated to Intel Mac CPU, Pi CPU, Hailo, or another simulator. There is no verified Pi/Mac throughput benchmark for our proposed 3D model; measure it. Wheel-biped jumping, exact WBR feasibility, accelerator purchase value, and research novelty remain unverified until robot parameters, matched literature review, and experiments exist.
